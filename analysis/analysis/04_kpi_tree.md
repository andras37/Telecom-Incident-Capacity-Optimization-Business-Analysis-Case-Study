# KPI Tree

## Purpose
The KPI tree defines how operational metrics are linked to business outcomes and decision-making in the context of incident management and capacity optimization.

The objective is not to measure everything, but to identify **which metrics matter for prioritization and intervention decisions**.

---

## Outcome Level (Business Impact)
These KPIs represent the business-relevant outcomes that leadership ultimately cares about.

- **SLA Compliance Rate**
  - Proxy for service reliability and contractual performance
- **Customer Impact Score (Proxy)**
  - Derived from repeat incidents, long resolution times, and severity
- **Operational Cost Pressure (Proxy)**
  - Indicated by overtime, backlog growth, and rework

These outcome metrics are influenced by multiple operational drivers and cannot be improved directly without addressing root causes.

---

## Control Level (Performance Management)
These KPIs help monitor and control operational performance and act as levers between activity and outcomes.

- **Average Resolution Time**
- **SLA Breach Frequency**
- **Backlog Size**
- **Team Capacity Utilization (Proxy)**

Control KPIs are actionable and can be influenced through staffing, prioritization rules, and process adjustments.

---

## Input Level (Operational Drivers)
These metrics describe workload and complexity drivers entering the system.

- **Incident Volume**
- **Incident Severity**
- **Incident Category Distribution**
- **Reopen Rate**
- **Repeat Incidents**

Input KPIs explain *why* control metrics move and help identify where bottlenecks originate.

---

## Decision Mapping
The KPI tree supports the following key decisions:

- Where to allocate limited support capacity
- Which incident categories should be prioritized for process improvement
- Whether SLA breaches are driven by volume, complexity, or capacity constraints
- Which improvement actions are likely to deliver the highest business impact

---

## BA Perspective
From a Business Analysis standpoint, the KPI tree:
- Prevents metric overload
- Creates a shared language across stakeholders
- Links operational data to business consequences
- Enables structured prioritization instead of reactive decision-making
