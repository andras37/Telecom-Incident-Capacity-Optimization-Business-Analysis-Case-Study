# Stakeholder Map

## Overview
This analysis identifies the key stakeholder groups involved in incident management and capacity-related decisions within the telecommunications organization.

Each stakeholder group has distinct objectives, success metrics, and decision drivers, which can create tension when prioritizing improvement initiatives.

---

## Key Stakeholders

### Operations Management
**Primary interest:** Operational efficiency and workload balance  
**Key concerns:**
- Uneven team utilization
- Backlogs and delayed resolutions
- Team performance accountability  

**Success indicators:**
- Stable resolution times
- Balanced workload across teams
- Reduced operational escalations

---

### Service Delivery / SLA Owners
**Primary interest:** SLA compliance and contractual obligations  
**Key concerns:**
- SLA breach frequency and severity
- Risk of penalties or reputational impact
- Lack of early warning indicators  

**Success indicators:**
- High SLA compliance rate
- Predictable service performance
- Clear ownership of breach drivers

---

### Finance
**Primary interest:** Cost efficiency and return on improvement efforts  
**Key concerns:**
- Rising operational costs (overtime, rework)
- Unclear ROI of process or capacity changes
- Budget constraints  

**Success indicators:**
- Cost reduction or stabilization
- Measurable impact from improvement initiatives
- Prioritized investments with clear justification

---

### Customer Experience
**Primary interest:** Customer satisfaction and retention  
**Key concerns:**
- Repeated incidents affecting the same customers
- Long resolution times impacting perception
- Lack of linkage between operational issues and churn risk  

**Success indicators:**
- Reduced repeat incidents
- Improved customer sentiment
- Lower churn risk indicators

---

## Stakeholder Tensions and Trade-offs
- Operations may prioritize workload balance, while SLA owners focus on breach reduction, even if it increases team pressure.
- Finance may resist capacity expansion without clear evidence of business impact.
- Customer Experience may push for improvements that are operationally expensive but strategically important.

---

## BA Perspective
The role of the Business Analyst in this context is to:
- Translate operational metrics into business-relevant insights
- Make trade-offs explicit rather than implicit
- Support prioritization decisions that balance cost, service quality, and customer impact
